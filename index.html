Moving the route planning algorithm to the client, focusing servers on delivering data

Linked Connections lets a browser or an app download public transport timetable data and calculate routes itself. The server publishes the data; the client runs the route planning algorithm.

Read the specification → Explore the data →

Benefits of client-side route planning

Control over routing features

Build the routing features your app needs, with your own preferences and optimisation criteria. Because the algorithm runs in your client, you control how journeys are calculated.

Route queries stay in your browser

Your browser calculates the route locally. It downloads timetable data without sending your origin, destination, or preferences as a route planning query to the server.

Unlimited route calculations

Users can calculate routes as often as they want. Routing does not require a paid API call for each query, so a successful app is not penalised with a growing bill for route calculations.

Publishing departure–arrival pairs over HTTP

A connection describes a vehicle’s departure from one stop and arrival at the next, with a time for each.

Linked Connections orders departure–arrival pairs by departure time and splits them into pages served over HTTP. Links let a client discover the next page and gather the connections it needs to plan a journey.
